# ADR 0013 — The compositor: libwayland-server inside siriusd, clients as desktop windows, pseudo-terminals

**Status:** accepted · 2026-10-11 · **milestone:** M8

## Context
M8 makes `siriusd` a Wayland compositor. The exit test: `weston-simple-shm`
and `weston-terminal` render and accept input inside windows the ImGui shell
decorates. ADR 0004 already ruled out wlroots and chose to serve the protocol
from `siriusd` itself; M6 ported libwayland and passed its test suite. What
was left open was how much of the server to write, how a client's window fits
a desktop whose window manager is Dear ImGui, and what else the two programs
need of the system -- which turned out to include a pseudo-terminal.

## Decisions

### libwayland-server does the protocol; siriusd implements the interfaces
The plan considered writing the wire marshalling from the protocol XML
instead of porting libwayland. The port exists and passes all 141 of its own
tests, so `siriusd` links `libwayland-server` and implements the interfaces
over it (`user/siriusd/wayland/`): `wl_compositor`, `wl_surface`, `wl_region`,
`wl_output`, `wl_seat` with pointer and keyboard, `xdg_wm_base` with
toplevels, popups and positioners, `zxdg_decoration_manager_v1`, and an
inert `wl_data_device_manager`. `wl_shm` is libwayland's own. The code for
the two `xdg` protocols is generated by `wayland-scanner` from XML kept in
the tree, so the compositor builds the same way for Sirius and for the host
harness, against whichever libwayland is there.

*Rejected:* a marshaller of our own -- a few thousand lines to re-earn a
test suite that already passes.

### A commit copies the buffer; the copy is what is drawn
On `wl_surface.commit` the client's shm buffer is copied into memory of the
compositor's and released at once. The desktop redraws the whole screen each
frame from that copy, so a client never has a buffer held hostage by a slow
frame and may draw its next one straight away. Damage is ignored. The
rasterizer learned the two pixel layouts clients draw (`wl_shm` ARGB8888,
premultiplied, and XRGB8888, both BGRA in memory) and blits them one texel
per pixel without unpremultiplying.

### A client's toplevel is one more desktop window
`shell/app.h`'s `App` gained what a window owned by another process needs:
hooks around `Begin()`, a close request that may be declined, a minimize
request, a name for the status line. A mapped toplevel becomes an `App`
whose contents are the surface, and Dear ImGui draws its title bar, border
and close button like any other window's. The size goes both ways: the
window follows what the client commits; a drag of its edge sends a
configure, and if the client does not follow within half a second of the
button coming up, the window goes back.

Clients are told so through `xdg-decoration`: every toplevel is offered
server-side decorations. Weston's toolkit does not ask, so the port patches
it to (ports/recipes/weston-clients): without that, its windows had two
title bars.

Popups are Dear ImGui windows of their own, kept in front of everything,
so that Dear ImGui's idea of what is under the pointer includes them.

### Input follows Dear ImGui's view of the desktop
Every frame, the window showing a surface reports whether the pointer is
over the client's part of it -- Dear ImGui knows whether the window is on
top, and whether the pointer is on a resize handle instead -- and whether it
has the focus. The seat then moves focus and sends the frame's motion,
buttons and wheel. A press on a client grabs the pointer for it until every
button is up; a press anywhere else belongs to Dear ImGui. Keys reach the
focused client as the raw evdev codes they arrive as, through an observer
the platform backend now offers.

The keymaps clients get are generated once by `xkbcli` (US, and German
without dead keys, matching the desktop's own layouts) and compiled into
`siriusd`; the compositor computes the modifier masks itself rather than
linking xkbcommon.

### Frame callbacks are paced
Callbacks are answered after the frame that showed the surface, and no
more often than every 16 ms. A client that redraws the moment it is told
(simple-shm does) otherwise had the desktop drawing a thousand frames a
second. A client waiting on a callback keeps the desktop from sleeping;
one that is minimized waits.

### The kernel grew pseudo-terminals
`weston-terminal` runs its shell with `forkpty()`. ADR 0011 deferred
pseudo-terminals to the first program that needed one; this is it.
`SYS_OPENPTY` makes both ends at once (`kernel/ipc/pty.cpp`), with a line
discipline between them: canonical and raw input, echo, erase, kill, word
erase, end of file, `VMIN`/`VTIME`, CR/NL translation, a window size, and
Ctrl+C, Ctrl+\ and Ctrl+Z as signals to the foreground process group. That
needed sessions and process groups: `setsid`, `setpgid`, `getsid`,
`getpgid`, `TIOCSCTTY`, `TIOCSPGRP`, and `kill()` of a group. Termios records
and ioctl numbers are Linux's, as mlibc's are (ADR 0007).

*Rejected:* `/dev/ptmx` and `/dev/pts`. devfs has fixed nodes and no notion
of an open that makes a new object; mlibc's `openpty` takes a sysdep that
makes the pair directly, and nothing on Sirius needs the slave's name.

### The clients are ported as they are, with their own build
The ports tree gained wayland-protocols, pixman, libpng, libxkbcommon,
cairo and the two weston clients. Weston's meson build will not configure
without libinput, libdrm and udev, which only its compositor needs, so a
patch adds a Makefile that builds just the clients, from the same file for
Sirius and for the host. cairo has no FreeType or fontconfig: the terminal's
text is cairo's built-in stroked font.

mlibc's Sirius sysdeps gained `fallocate` (grow the file) and `msync`
(nothing to flush), both of which libwayland calls.

## Consequences
- `make image ENTRY=9 && ./tools/check-compositor.sh` is the exit test:
  both clients start from the panel, simple-shm animates, keys typed in the
  terminal run commands, the terminal is moved and resized and simple-shm
  closed by its close button. `tools/check-desktop.sh` and libwayland's
  suite still pass; `/bin/ptytest` (30 checks) runs at boot with the others.
- `make harness` runs the compositor on Linux: Linux builds of Wayland
  clients started from its desktop open their windows in it.
- Only `wl_shm` buffers: no dmabuf, no GPU clients. No subsurfaces, no
  viewporter, no fractional scaling, one output.
- Copy and paste between clients does nothing yet; neither does drag and
  drop.
- Every commit copies the whole buffer, and every frame redraws every
  window: a large animating client costs a full-window copy and blit per
  frame.
- The terminal's font is legible, not pretty. FreeType, fontconfig and a
  font are the next ports.
- Pseudo-terminals have no job control beyond signals: no `SIGTTIN` or
  `SIGTTOU`, no `/dev/tty`. `kill(-1)` still means the caller alone.
- A static `weston-terminal` is 3.3 MB, close to the kernel's 4 MiB limit
  on an executable it reads whole.
